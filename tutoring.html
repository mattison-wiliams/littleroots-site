About me

I taught 7th and 8th grade science at Centennial Middle School for two years. Before that, I spent a year subbing in Lake Stevens and Snohomish, including a long-term middle school science position, and a year as a K to 5 math intervention specialist. I stepped away from the classroom when I had my daughter.

Today I run Little Roots Forest School, a nature-based class for toddlers and their grown-ups.

My goal is to make learning accessible and engaging for every kid. I want to help them love learning and stay curious about the world.

More about my background

Middle school science

At Centennial Middle School, I taught 7th and 8th grade science using the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), which Washington schools use, and the OpenSciEd and Sprocket curriculums. Class was hands-on, with labs, projects, science notebooks, group work, and lots of discussion.

Math intervention

I traveled on a cart to every classroom, so I taught every single student in the school. That year, the staff gave me the Golden Apple Award for dedication and service.

I taught each whole class, then pulled small groups while the rest of the class played math games, which took a lot of behavior management too. We always started with a number talk. I love number talks for building kids’ math confidence and mental math skills.

I used i-Ready data to plan that instruction, so I’m comfortable reading i-Ready reports and can use your child’s scores to plan our sessions.

Preschool and student teaching

From 2018 to 2021, I taught 3 to 5 year olds at A Little Darling School in Bellingham under my mentor, Netta Darling. We covered early reading, math, social studies, and art, and planned around each child’s interests.

I also taught social and emotional skills every day. Netta’s philosophy still guides how I teach: the environment is the teacher, let children lead, and trust the process.

I student taught in 1st grade at Parkview Elementary in Bellingham. I also led a science club for 4th and 5th graders and cotaught a K to 5 fitness club.

Education and training

I earned my degree in Elementary Education with a focus in general sciences from Western Washington University, graduating cum laude.

How I teach

I love using hands-on materials. In math, kids work with manipulatives like coins and counting cubes, so they can see how the math works. We also use whiteboards and games.

I also work study skills into whatever we’re doing. If your child has a big project coming up, we might break it into smaller steps together and write them in their planner.

I build sessions around what your child is into. If they love sharks, we might compare shark lengths to practice measuring, or read about how sharks find their food.

I love giving kids something to look forward to. In my 8th grade classroom, we did Phenomena Friday. I’d show the class something surprising from the real world, they’d work out an explanation, and the next week I’d reveal what was going on and share a new one. In tutoring, every session ends with a “wonder,” a surprising question we answer next time.

When something feels hard, I model a growth mindset and help kids find the words for what they’re feeling. My mascot is the axolotl, which can regrow its limbs, the same way we can regrow our learning.

Tardigrades are tiny animals that can survive almost anything. Each kid colors one to tape into their tutoring notebook, and when things get tough, we look back at it and remember that we’re tardigrade tough.

I’m happy to talk with your child’s classroom teacher, if your family would like, so we’re working on the same things.

What tutoring looks like

Each session

Every session follows the same rhythm, so your child always knows what to expect. We start with a check-in about how they’re feeling and what’s new at school, then review what we worked on last time. From there:

  • Math: a number talk, where we solve a problem in our heads and talk about how we saw it, then practice the skill, often with manipulatives, and finish with a math game.
  • Reading: we read together, practice the skill we’re working on, and finish with a literacy game. Younger readers do phonics practice like word sorts and word chains. Older readers work on understanding what they read, vocabulary, and tackling longer words.
  • Writing: we talk through ideas first, plan with a simple organizer, and write a little at a time. Then we go back and make one part stronger, so writing feels doable instead of overwhelming.

At the end, your child rates how confident they feel, colors in their goal tracker, and gets their wonder for next time. If you choose a 1 hour session, we can split the time between two subjects.

Each month

We follow the same four-week cycle every month, so we’re always checking progress and adjusting.

Week 1

Finding the starting line

I use a few short, low-pressure activities to see where your child is, and I watch how they get their answers, since that tells me more than right or wrong. Then we set a goal together.

Weeks 2 and 3

Building the skill

We work on one skill at a time, in small steps, with quick checks along the way so I can adjust as we go. I show the skill first, then we practice it together until your child can do it on their own.

Week 4

Progress check

I give a similar version of the Week 1 activities so we can compare, and your child sees their growth side by side in their notebook. Then we decide together whether to keep working on this goal or set a new one.

Week 1 activities might look like this. In math, your child might sort facts into “I just know it,” “I can figure it out,” and “Not yet.” In reading, they might read a short passage and a list of words that shows which sound patterns they know. In writing, they might write a few sentences about something they love.

Some goals take longer than a month at one session a week. Kids who come twice a week often move faster.

Goals

Every goal is built around your child, and they help write it.

See example goals

For younger kids (K to 3)

Goals are written in kid-friendly words, with “I can” steps they check off along the way. Behind the scenes, I track a measurable version to share with you.

  • Reading: “I am learning to read words with silent e.” Steps like “I can tell when an e is silent” and “I can read a silent e story to my family.”
  • Math: “I am learning to multiply by 2, 5, and 10.” Steps like “I can skip-count by 5s” and “I can show a multiplication problem with cubes.”
  • Writing: “I am learning to write a story with a beginning, middle, and end.” Steps like “I can plan my three parts” and “I can use words like first, next, and last.”

For older kids (4 to 8)

We write SMART goals together: specific, measurable, realistic, and with a timeline. Learning to set their own goals is a skill they’ll use for years.

  • Math: “In the next 6 weeks, I will add and subtract fractions with different denominators, getting at least 8 out of 10 right and explaining my steps.”
  • Language arts: “In the next 4 weeks, I will read an article and write a 2 to 3 sentence summary with the main idea and two key details.”
  • Science: “By the end of this unit, I will write a scientific explanation with a claim, evidence, and reasoning, and score a 3 out of 4 on my teacher’s rubric.”
  • Study skills: “For the next 4 weeks, I will write every assignment in my planner and check it before bed at least 4 nights a week.”

Staying in touch and tracking progress

After each session, I’ll send you a quick text or email, whichever you prefer, about what we worked on and how it went. Once a month, I’ll send a longer update showing where your child started, where they are now, and one easy way to help at home.

Assessment looks different for each family, depending on your child’s school or homeschool setup. Along with my own checks, we can look at:

  • School work and scores. Report cards and any test scores from school, as much as you’re comfortable sharing. Those scores aren’t always accurate, since kids sometimes rush through the tests, so my own checks help fill in the picture.
  • Their tutoring notebook, which shows their work over time.

How it works

We’ll start with a free 15 minute consultation by phone, or we can go back and forth by email if you’d prefer, to see if we’re a good fit. There’s no commitment, so you can try a first session and see how it goes. If it’s not the right fit, no hard feelings. Let me know which days and times work for you and we’ll find a time.

We can meet on Zoom, at your home, or at a library, whatever is easiest for your family. In person sessions are available within about 10 miles of the Everett Mall, including Everett, Mill Creek, Mukilteo, Lynnwood, Snohomish, and Bothell.

I’m also happy to work with two kids from the same family together if they’re at similar levels, or a small group of friends. Another option is two kids back to back, 30 minutes each. Tell me what you have in mind and we’ll figure out what works for your kids.

Payment and cancellations

There are no signup fees, and you pay one session at a time. Payment is due before each session through a secure Stripe link I’ll send you. If you need to cancel, please let me know at least 24 hours ahead. Sessions canceled with less notice are charged in full.

If your child is sick, just let me know and we’ll reschedule at no charge. If I ever need to cancel, you won’t be charged and we’ll find a new time.
