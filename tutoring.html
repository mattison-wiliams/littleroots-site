About me

I taught 7th and 8th grade science at Centennial Middle School for three years. Before that, I spent a year as a K to 5 math intervention specialist.

Today I run Little Roots Forest School, a nature-based class for toddlers and their grown-ups. I’m also a mom, and I’m CPR and First Aid certified. As a licensed teacher, I’ve passed a fingerprint background check.

My goal is to make learning accessible and engaging for every kid, and to help them love learning and stay curious about the world.

More about my background

Middle school science

At Centennial Middle School, I taught 7th and 8th grade science using the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), which Washington schools use, and the OpenSciEd curriculum. Class was hands-on, with labs, projects, science notebooks, group work, and lots of discussion.

Math intervention

I spent a year as a K to 5 math intervention specialist at Eagleridge Elementary in Ferndale, and I only left that job because I moved. It worked like music, PE, or library. I traveled on a cart to every classroom, so I taught every single student in the school. That year, the staff gave me the Golden Apple Award for dedication and service.

I taught each whole class, then pulled small groups while the rest of the class played math games, which took a lot of behavior management too. We always started with a number talk. I love number talks for building kids’ math confidence and mental math skills.

I used i-Ready data to plan that instruction, so I’m comfortable reading i-Ready reports and can use your child’s scores to plan our sessions.

Preschool and student teaching

From 2018 to 2021, I taught 3 to 5 year olds at A Little Darling School in Bellingham under my mentor, Netta Darling. We covered early reading, math, social studies, and art, and planned around each child’s interests.

I also taught social and emotional skills every day. Netta’s philosophy still guides how I teach: the environment is the teacher, let children lead, and trust the process.

I student taught in 1st grade at Parkview Elementary in Bellingham. I also led a science club for 4th and 5th graders and cotaught a K to 5 fitness club.

Education and training

I earned my degree in Elementary Education from Western Washington University, graduating cum laude.

My language arts lessons follow the science of reading, and in every subject I use evidence-based teaching methods. I’m GLAD certified, which is training in strategies that support kids who are learning English.

How I teach

Every session starts with a check in. I ask how your child is feeling and if there’s anything new going on with their learning. Then we warm up by reviewing what we worked on last time. From there it depends on the subject.

  • Math: we do a number talk, practice the skill we’re working on, and finish with a game.
  • Language arts: we read together and do some targeted phonics practice, like word sorts and word chains, before a game at the end.
  • More than one subject? If you choose a 1 hour session, we can split the time between them.

I love using hands-on materials. In math, kids work with things like coins and counting cubes, so they can see how the math works. We also use whiteboards and games. For in person sessions, I bring everything we need.

I also work study skills into whatever we’re doing. If your child has a big project coming up, we might break it into smaller steps together and write them in their planner.

I build sessions around what your child is into. If they love sharks, we might compare shark lengths to practice measuring, or read about how sharks find their food.

I love giving kids something to look forward to. In my 8th grade classroom, we did Phenomena Friday. I’d show the class something surprising from the real world, they’d work out an explanation, and the next week I’d reveal what was going on and share a new one. I bring that same sense of wonder to tutoring.

When something feels hard, I model a growth mindset and help kids find the words for what they’re feeling. My mascot is the axolotl, which can regrow its limbs, the same way we can regrow our learning.

Tardigrades are tiny animals that can survive almost anything. Each kid colors one to tape into their tutoring notebook, and when things get tough, we look back at it and remember that we’re tardigrade tough.

I’m happy to talk with your child’s classroom teacher, if your family would like, so we’re working on the same things.

Staying in touch and tracking progress

After each session, I’ll send you a quick text or email, whichever you prefer, about what we worked on and how it went. Once a month, I’ll send a longer progress update by email.

Assessment looks different for each family, depending on your child’s school or homeschool setup.

Here’s how we’ll know it’s working:

  • SMART goals. You, your child, and I set goals together that are specific, measurable, and have a timeline, so progress is easy to see. I also teach kids how to set goals for themselves.
  • Checking against the start. I do my own assessment when we begin, then check again every few weeks to compare.
  • School work and scores. We can look at school work, report cards, and i-Ready or Star scores together, as much as you’re comfortable sharing. Those scores aren’t always accurate, since kids sometimes rush through the tests, so my own checks help fill in the picture.
  • Their tutoring notebook, which shows their work over time.

How it works

We’ll start with a free 15 minute consultation by phone or email, whichever you prefer, to see if we’re a good fit. From there, try a first session and see how it goes. You pay one session at a time, so there’s no commitment. Let me know which days and times work for you and we’ll find a time.

We can meet on Zoom, at your home, or at a library, whatever is easiest for your family. In person sessions are available within about 10 miles of the Everett Mall, including Everett, Mill Creek, Mukilteo, Lynnwood, Snohomish, and Bothell.

I’m also happy to work with two kids from the same family together if they’re at similar levels, or a small group of friends. Another option is two kids back to back, 30 minutes each. Tell me what you have in mind and we’ll figure out what works for your kids.

Why in person costs more: it’s the most effective way I can teach. Sitting right next to your child, I can observe so much more of how they’re thinking, like how they hold their pencil or what they’re counting on, and help right away. I also come to you and bring all the hands-on materials.

Siblings or friends together: add $10 per extra kid for every 30 minutes, for groups of up to 4.

For younger kids (K to 2), I recommend in person. Little ones tend to focus better with no screen in the way. A full hour works well for them too, since we mix in movement breaks and switch between activities. Online can work well for older kids.

Have something else in mind, like a different session length or a mix of online and in person? Just ask.

These are founding rates for the first 3 families. After that, prices will go up for new families, but founding families keep this rate for as long as they tutor with me. Siblings count as one family.

Payment and cancellations

There are no signup fees, and you pay one session at a time. Payment is due before each session through a secure Stripe link I’ll send you. If you need to cancel, please let me know at least 24 hours ahead. Sessions canceled with less notice are charged in full.

If your child is sick, just let me know and we’ll reschedule at no charge. If I ever need to cancel, you won’t be charged and we’ll find a new time.

Why autumn is a good time to start

First report cards and parent-teacher conferences often show where a child is having a hard time. Starting in autumn gives you the rest of the school year to work on it.
