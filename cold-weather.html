Cold and Wet Weather

How to Dress Tips Stations If We Cancel

We meet rain or shine, all year. Here’s how we set up the morning when it’s cold or wet, and what helps families stay comfortable.

How to dress

  1. 1

    Base layer

    Wool or synthetic. Cotton gets cold once it’s wet.

  2. 2

    Mid layer

    Fleece or another warm layer.

  3. 3

    Outer layer

    Rain gear or a coat on top to block wind and rain.

  4. 4

    Accessories

    Hat, mittens, wool socks, and boots.

Tips for you

What to pack

  • Bring a blanket to sit on at circle and snack. Cold, damp ground pulls heat fast when kids are sitting still.
  • Pack two pairs of mittens: a waterproof pair for wet play, and a warm pair that slips on and off easily for when hands just get cold. A lot of young toddlers won’t keep waterproof mittens on. With my daughter, my plan is to let her go bare-handed for parts of the morning, then warm her hands up with the hand warmer trick below and put the warm pair back on. Our favorites are in the Gear Guide.
  • Pack two clean washcloths in a ziplock bag. We like one damp and one dry, but use whatever works for your family.
  • Bring a thermos of something warm for you and your child, like warm water or warm milk.
  • Keep hand warmers in your pocket, not your child’s. Disposable warmers get hotter than you’d expect, and little ones can’t always tell you when something’s too hot. They also shouldn’t end up in little mouths. I keep mine in my own pocket to warm up my hands or a spare pair of mittens, then hold my daughter’s hands or swap the warm mittens on. I skip rechargeable warmers too, since a popular brand was recalled in 2026 for overheating.

Keeping warm

  • Toddlers often won’t tell you they’re cold. If yours slows down or stops playing, check their hands and feet.
  • Dress yourself warm too. You’ll be sitting and crouching on the ground for a lot of the morning.
  • Introduce new cold weather pieces one at a time, like mittens one week and a hat the next, so they’re used to them by the time it’s really cold.

Babywearing

  • Skip the puffy down jacket on your baby. The bulk makes it hard to get a snug fit in the carrier. Down also only stays warm when it’s fluffed up with air, so once the carrier presses it flat, it stops doing its job.
  • Layer under one big coat. Dress your baby in a base layer and a fleece, then zip one big jacket or carrier cover around both of you. Your body heat helps keep them warm too.
  • Face them in. They can rest their head on your chest and share your warmth, and it’s cozier for them.
  • Add a warm hat, and keep their face uncovered so you can always see it and check their breathing.

How we set up the stations

  • I fill the water the morning of class so it’s still warm when kids use it. That includes the handwashing station.
  • We use a lot less water at the stations in the colder months. On dry days you’ll see oats, rice, and cornmeal for pouring and scooping, plus playdough, which doesn’t get too cold to use. On rainy days the mud kitchen switches to things that don’t mind getting wet, like pinecones, rocks, and organic coconut coir.
  • Rain becomes part of the stations. We paint with it, set out pots and tins to catch it, and look for puddles on the walk.
  • We set out chunky tools that are easy to grip with mittens on, like foam rollers and paint sticks. On cold days we skip the really messy setups so kids aren’t washing their hands over and over. Extra washcloths are always out.
  • Circle stays short on cold mornings. Little bodies cool down when they stop moving, so we get up and walking sooner.
