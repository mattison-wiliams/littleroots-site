What Is Little Roots?

Little Roots Forest School is a weekly outdoor class where toddlers and their grown-ups explore, play, and learn together in the forest. We meet Wednesday mornings at McCollum Park in Everett, rain or shine, because some of our best days happen in the mud.

What a Morning Looks Like

Times are approximate. We follow a rhythm, not a clock.

9:30

Arrival & Free Explore

Stations are already set up when you walk in. Your child can start exploring right away while everyone settles in.

9:45

Opening Circle

A short five minutes together. We ring the chime, sing our name song so every child hears their name, and take a quick look at the day. There’s also a question of the day for the grown-ups, like “What is a hobby of yours, even if you haven’t gotten to do it lately?” Many great conversations and connections have started with these questions. Then it’s right back to the stations.

9:50

Discovery Stations

Six to eight stations set up to explore, like the mud kitchen, playdough, sensory bins, collaborative and individual art, and small world trays. Your child moves freely between them. Nobody tells them where to go or when to switch.

10:20

Snack Time

Snack is family provided, so bring a nut-free snack from home. We keep a handwashing station with soap and offer hand sanitizer, so you can clean hands before snack.

10:30

Morning Gathering

Eating right into circle is totally fine. A snacking toddler is often a happy, settled one. We start by singing Open, Shut Them, then settle in for a short story picked around the day’s nature focus. After the story we say good morning to Pip, our class chickadee finger puppet and a group favorite. Then a movement song or two to shake out the wiggles before we head for the trail.

10:45

Nature Walk

We head out on the forest loop at toddler pace, a short walk so little legs stay happy. We walk the same trail every week on purpose. When kids return to the same place over and over, they start to notice what is different. Each week starts with a guiding question, like “What is falling?” or “Who lives under here?” Depending on the week, we might bring hand lenses, collection cups, or the nature frames the kids decorated at the stations.

10:55

Closing Circle

We circle up right on the trail, sing The More We Get Together with ASL signs, and say goodbye to the forest together.
